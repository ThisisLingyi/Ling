Unifying Data Management across two product lines with Jobs To Be Done

Sole domain UXR · Splunk Data Management · 6-month foundational program
Lingyi Ge · Sole domain researcher · JTBD · cross-product synthesis · gap analysis
Six-month roadmap · 6 min read
Summary

I unified fragmented research across two product lines, discovered what should be shared versus product-specific, and created a scoring system that made foundational JTBD research usable in planning. As the sole Data Management researcher spanning Splunk Core and Observability, I ran a six-month program that turned parallel job maps into one shared job language, with forked execution paths and a repeatable path from evidence to prioritization.

01 · The organizational problem

Why this research needed to exist

After the AppDynamics integration, Splunk wanted a unified Data Management experience, but Core and Observability teams were working from separate research narratives. Customers experienced one domain; internally, teams lacked a shared understanding of the jobs that should, or should not, be unified.

The consequence was concrete: duplicated interviews, conflicting prioritization, and the risk of a compromised “unified” UX that served neither forwarder-first nor OTel-first workflows.

02 · My mandate and approach

What I owned

As the sole researcher spanning both product lines, I led a six-month foundational JTBD program: comparable job maps for Core and Observability, cross-product synthesis, and a prioritization system built on the findings. I interviewed admins, engineers, architects, and security stakeholders, then moved the library through three phases, Foundation → Alignment → Action, socialized in monthly DM share-outs.

03 · The turning-point insight

Shared job, forked workflows

Once both maps existed, comparison, not assertion, made the strategy credible. Core and Observability customers shared the same ultimate job and many emotional outcomes, but their little jobs diverged where technical workflows differed.

Shared ultimate job

Ensure the right organizational data is available in the platform for search, analytics, and operations, reliably and cost-effectively.

One concrete shared little job

Separate signal from noise before ingestion, so teams aren’t paying to index low-value telemetry.

Where the paths diverged mattered just as much:

DimensionSplunk CoreObservability
Data typesLogs, metrics (limited)Metrics, distributed traces
Connection methodsUniversal / heavy forwarderOpenTelemetry
Agent managementLimited native toolingRicher agent lifecycle
Product implication

Unification required one job language with distinct execution paths, not one onboarding flow for everyone. The ultimate-job narrative should be unified; little jobs and journeys must fork where technical reality diverges.

04 · Making the research actionable

Beyond a job map

Job maps alone don’t create action. I introduced a gap-analysis framework that evaluated current product support, missing capabilities, user satisfaction, and ownership for each little job. Opportunity scoring then let PM, Design, and Engineering discuss investment using the same evidence.

Worked example

Users needed to separate signal from noise before ingestion, but controls arrived too late. The job scored low on satisfaction and became an explicit planning input for pre-ingestion filtering and cost-preview exploration.

05 · What changed

What became possible

Research contribution and observed planning use, not a product roadmap commitment.

01

Established a shared Data Management job narrative across Core and Observability.

02

Reframed unification as shared outcomes with appropriately forked workflows.

03

Created a repeatable path from research evidence to cross-functional planning.

06 · Reflection

Comparison, not assertion, made the unification strategy credible. If I repeated the program, I would introduce gap scoring earlier so teams could connect foundational research to planning sooner.
